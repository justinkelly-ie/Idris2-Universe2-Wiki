# Law 3: The Discrete Casimir Effect & Vacuum Boundary Mode Density

## 1. Physical Principle & Constructive Foundation
In classical quantum field theory, calculating the Casimir effect between two conducting plates leads to an infinite divergent sum over zero-point vacuum modes ($\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{2}\hbar \omega_n = \infty$). Continuum physics bypasses this divergence through non-constructive zeta-function regularization ($\zeta(-1) = -1/12$).

In our constructivist multiset framework, space is a discrete 3-torus, and a cavity of integer width $d \in \mathbb{N}^+$ acts as a **finite multiset capacity filter**.

The allowed standing modes inside the cavity form a finite discrete sequence $k \in \{1, \dots, d\}$, yielding the exact triangular mode energy:
$$E(d) = \sum_{k=1}^d k = \frac{d(d+1)}{2}$$

## 2. Discrete Casimir Attraction
When the boundary plate separation contracts from $d$ to $d-1$, the highest boundary mode $[d]$ is squeezed out of the cavity into the unconfined bulk:
$$\Delta E = E(d-1) - E(d) = \frac{(d-1)d}{2} - \frac{d(d+1)}{2} = -d < 0$$

The Casimir force is strictly negative (attractive) and exact across all discrete plate separations.
